Why Spaced Repetition Works

A memory fades along a forgetting curve: the chance you can recall it, R(t) = e−t/S, slides toward zero as days pass. Each well-timed review raises the memory's stability S — flattening the curve and stretching the gap before you need to see the card again. Review just as recall dips to your target, and the next interval grows the most.

Higher target → review sooner & more often; lower → fewer, riskier reviews.

What's happening

The blue curves are successive forgetting curves, drawn as a ladder. The first card is fragile — low stability S, so R(t) = e−t/S plunges within days. The dashed line is your target retention: the moment a curve crosses it, the card is due.

Each review multiplies stability (FSRS-lite: S × 1.8, with a bonus when you review right at the target, where the spacing effect is strongest). Because the next interval is −S·ln(retention), a bigger S means a visibly flatter curve and a longer wait — the same algorithm that drives atlas's study scheduler. Keep reviewing: the curve barely droops and the interval climbs from days to weeks to months.
